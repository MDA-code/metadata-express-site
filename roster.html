How to make a roster

About 10 minutes, once per team per season.

Your roster is what lets you type 23 and get Marcus Ellis. It's a list with one person per line: the jersey number and the name, exactly as you want it written into your photos and clips. The quickest way is to make it right in Metadata Express, which keeps it in your Saved Rosters. You can also bring in a text file.

1. Make it in Metadata Express

From the team's website (fastest)

Click From a Link next to Roster in Session Setup (or Choose Roster › From a Link in Label Clips) and paste the team's roster page (it usually ends in /roster). Most college athletics sites work, plus ESPN team rosters in any league, NFL team sites, NBA.com, NHL.com, MLB.com and MLS (mlssoccer.com and club sites). Metadata Express fills in the numbers and names, with offense, defense and special-teams letters for football. Check the names it lists, add your coaches and staff, click Save Roster and give it a name.

Type or paste it

Click Choose Roster › Create a Roster. Type each jersey number and name, or paste two columns copied from a spreadsheet. It warns you about a missing or repeated number, and it works without internet. Click Save Roster and give it a name, like Riverside Hawks Football 2026. There is no file to keep track of.

2. Saved Rosters

Every roster you create, make from a link or pick from your computer is kept in the Saved Rosters tab and at the top of the Choose Roster menu, for photos and for clips. From the list you can use one, edit it, rename it, delete it, or save a copy as a text file for someone else.

  • A new season: put the season in the roster's name. When the team changes, select the roster and click New Season. You get a copy named for next year to change, and last year's stays as it was for older games.
  • Update from Link: a roster made from a team's page can read the page again. It shows who is new, who is gone and who changed number before you save, and it keeps the spellings you fixed.
  • Share it with your team: choose More › Share in a Template to put the roster in a template file. Whoever loads the template gets the roster, how its players are written and its codes.
  • Roster files stay where they are: a text file you pick from your computer or a team server is only remembered in the list, so changes a teammate makes to it reach you.

3. Jersey numbers, and people without one

WhoWhat to typeExample
A player with a unique numberJust the number23 Marcus Ellis
Two players who share a number (common in football)Number plus o (offense), d (defense) or s (special teams)11o Isaiah Coleman
11d Andre Whitfield Jr.
Coaches, staff, anyone without a numberA short code of letters you'll rememberhc head coach, oc offensive coordinator, ad athletic director

Every line needs its own number or code. If two lines share one, the later one wins.

Brands and sponsors (optional)

Want to find every photo with the Nike or Gatorade logo later? Check the Brand box on its row (in a text file, put it under a Brands: heading). Brands are saved as keywords you can search for, but never named in the caption.

4. Spell the names exactly right

The name is written into every photo exactly as you type it here, so it should match how the athlete is listed wherever your photos go: the team's athlete-delivery or photo-sharing platform, the official roster, your newsroom style.

  • Include suffixes: Jr., III.
  • Keep hyphens, apostrophes and accents: Smith-Jones, O'Connor, José.
  • Use the name the athlete actually goes by if that's how they're signed up, for example DJ rather than a full first name.

Why this matters: if your team sends photos to athletes automatically by name, a photo tagged "Andre Whitfield" won't reach someone signed up as "Andre Whitfield Jr." Copy the names straight from that platform's athlete list if you can.

5. Or bring in a text file

A roster can also be a plain text file, made in any editor or spreadsheet. Choose it with Choose Roster › A Roster File and it joins your Saved Rosters.

91s   Luis Ortega

Coaches and staff:
hc    Ray Simmons
oc    Dana Whitlock
ad    Karen Lopez

Brands:
nk    Nike
gt    Gatorade
  • One person per line: the number or code, then a Tab, space or comma, then the name.
  • Headings and blank lines are ignored, so lines like Players: and Coaches and staff: are fine for keeping things organized.
  • Capital letters don't matter in what you type: HC and hc are the same.

On a Mac (TextEdit)

  1. Open TextEdit and choose File › New.
  2. Choose Format › Make Plain Text (important: rich text won't work).
  3. Type or paste one person per line.
  4. File › Save, name it something like Hawks Football 2026.txt, and keep the encoding as Unicode (UTF-8).

On Windows (Notepad)

  1. Open Notepad, type or paste one person per line.
  2. File › Save As, choose UTF-8 encoding, and save as a .txt file.

From a spreadsheet (Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets)

  1. Put the jersey numbers in column A and the names in column B, with no other columns.
  2. Either copy both columns and paste them into Create a Roster in the app (or into TextEdit or Notepad),
  3. or export it as CSV (File › Export To › CSV in Numbers; File › Save As › CSV UTF-8 in Excel; File › Download › CSV in Google Sheets). Metadata Express opens .csv files too.

6. Using it in the app

  1. In Session Setup or Label Clips, click Choose Roster and pick it from your saved rosters. Metadata Express remembers it for next time.
  2. While tagging:
    • Type 23 and press Enter to add that player.
    • Type 11 when two players share it, and you'll get a quick choice; or type 11o to skip the choice.
    • Type hc for the head coach, or any code you made.
    • Type two or more letters of a name, like col, for suggestions.

Tips

  • Keep one roster per team per season, and update it when the roster changes (transfers, new numbers, walk-ons).
  • Add the people you photograph most even if they're not on the official roster: coaches, trainers, mascots, guests.
  • Typed a name or number that isn't on the roster? Metadata Express offers to add it, to that photo only or to your roster.
